Session Thirty --- 10/11/26

1832 AS --- Hephal 02

Respect Not Found For Killing A Dragon

There ought to be a customary period after killing a dragon during which nobody expects anything further of you.
Three days, perhaps. A decent bed. Several complimentary drinks. Someone sufficiently attractive asking whether the fighting was terribly frightening, and having the courtesy to believe whatever answer I provide.
We managed breakfast and some research.
Roon remains alive. I record this with considerable affection and the understanding that future historians may demand corroboration.

The Sorry State of the Library

The Monastery of the Hand has books to put back on shelves, wounds to tend, and rather more sky visible through parts of the premises than its builders intended. We have questions.
Hanedal. The Omnium Well. The wand. How to reach the first, find the second, recover the third, and remain sufficiently intact to appreciate the accomplishment.
Samuel offered what guidance he could. The eastern coast. A town where we might secure passage. An island whose dangers have had ample time to establish themselves without the inconvenience of visitors.
Bedrock’s warnings did little to improve the prospect. Much of Hanedal’s history has disappeared beneath war and neglect. Whatever now inhabits the place is unlikely to welcome six curious strangers carrying weapons and asking after its most valuable secret.
A familiar social difficulty. Usually I begin with a song.
Corvinas’s book offered something more substantial: Pogo Hammerstein’s account of a country that once taught people to use magic. People who had not been born with it. A queen drawing upon the power of Chaotix. Knowledge shared, wonders made, and eventually Nahundra arriving with the usual conviction that slaughter becomes respectable when one supplies the correct religious explanation.
That is what the account says.
I find myself increasingly particular about those words.
I know songs of Nahundra’s wars. I know the appetite of states that find their neighbours’ existence provocative. But I could summon no song that independently confirmed this queen and this history. Gildas ought to have inherited some account of such a place through the traditions of his own craft. A birthplace of wizardry should leave descendants who remember its name.
How does so much disappear?
War burns libraries. People die. Stories become unfashionable. I know all the ordinary answers.
I am carrying a book that obliges me to consider extraordinary ones.
For a performance, I can improve a siege, enlarge a dragon, or give a dying king a speech he lacked the breath to deliver. The audience understands the arrangement. They have purchased an evening.
Here, a wrong name could send us to the wrong ruin. A missing sentence could conceal the reason a kingdom died.
I want the words right.

Songs For the Silent Diners

Naturally, having spent the morning concerning myself with the preservation of truth, I spent part of the day performing.
The monastery’s dining hall proved an agreeable venue. I dedicated some music to those observing vows of silence. An audience constitutionally prevented from heckling has much to recommend it.
One monk departed. I choose to believe he required privacy to master his emotions.
I sang of Mihira. I also found occasion for the Off-Key Dragon, with Mikani offering the sort of assistance that makes a man appreciate the full breadth of friendship.
Gildas continues to show promise as a subject for a much longer composition. Grease alone deserves a verse. Levitation could sustain several, provided I arrange for the refrain to descend occasionally. After yesterday’s counterspells, I may need a chorus capable of interrupting the musicians.
We laughed.
I want that recorded too. We did not spend the entire day looking meaningfully at distant mountains. There was food, music, argument, and the pleasant business of irritating people who know us well enough to remain.
Eventually, we argued about leaving.
Roon preferred first light tomorrow. Others preferred immediately. We examined daylight, distance, weather, danger, and apparently every possible relationship among them.
Rayetta finally guided us through the Gale, pointing out signs I would have mistaken for more snow intending to kill me. We emerged near sunset without incident.
Roon maintained that arriving safely did not make the decision sound.
There is something deeply reassuring about a man who survives the journey and still intends to win the argument.

Beyond the Gale and Campfire Stories

We camped beyond the Gale.
Mikani told us of a friendly dragon from the stories of her childhood in the Celestial Isles. A dragon that helped her people. After our recent acquaintance with the species, I was willing to hear an alternative account.
I told The Silent Queen of Whispervale.
A woman loses her beloved and continues to govern. Her people still need decisions, protection, someone to receive their petitions. Grief does not clear her calendar. She rises and does what must be done.
It went well.
I know how to tell that story. Where to let the warmth linger. How long to leave the room quiet before she speaks again. The small details that make an audience care for a woman who never existed.
Some nights a familiar story requires rather more care than usual.
Corvinas followed with a tale of a saint, which we improved through an unnecessary discussion of pears. Then came the watches, and sleep.
I had hoped to spend mine unconscious.

Visions of Celestial Places

I found myself in a library.
Even now, writing that feels inadequate enough to be dishonest.
There were levels rising beyond any sensible architecture, books beyond counting, and angelic attendants moving among them. I was permitted to fly. Upward we went through all that knowledge, and for a little while I forgot to be afraid.
Imagine the histories.
The languages nobody living remembers. The original verses. Every place where some diligent copyist had decided an inconvenient word must surely have been a mistake.
I could have remained there a very long time.
We stopped at great double doors bearing a symbol I recognised from Father Joseph’s temple in Ventures.
Siath.
Beyond them sat a woman poring over a text. Beautiful, apparently young, and perhaps three times the size one ordinarily expects of a woman. Divinity evidently allows considerable freedom in these matters.
She noticed me.
There was something we needed to discuss.
“You have something that is mine.”
I told her I had something she had given me, which I had not necessarily wanted.
I have rehearsed rather grander accusations. In the event, that was what came out.
She said she had not given it to me.
I must write that plainly.
Siath said she did not give me the grimoire.
It had been stolen, or one of her angels had gone rogue. She did not know who had first taken it. Whoever possessed it before me had removed that knowledge from her.
From Siath.
I had spent the day wondering how a country’s history could disappear. That night, the goddess who should have known the answer to the theft of her own book told me the knowledge had been taken away.
I explained that I had received it. That I had assumed it came from her. That I had been bitter.
She believed me.
This should have been comforting. I am still trying to determine why it was not.
For so long my anger had somewhere definite to go. I could resent the hand that placed this burden upon me. I could address my complaints to someone, even if I expected no reply.
Now I must revise the name at the top of the letter.
The burden remains.
I told her I had tried to protect the book. I did not claim to be its ideal custodian. I have enough vanity to furnish a palace, but apparently there are limits.
I said I feared what would happen if it fell into the wrong hands.
She warned that someone beyond my companions might have overheard more than I intended.
I have been turning over conversations ever since.
Which room? Which voice beyond a doorway? Who was present whom I failed to count?
She said she trusted me with the book.
“For now.”
An endorsement admirably designed to prevent complacency.
After this was over, she could more readily retrieve it. Coming to the material plane now carried a danger even for her: should the calamity occur while she was there, she would lose her divinity.
I had no elegant response to that.
“Well, you probably don’t want that,” appears to have been my contribution.
Let future theologians work with what they have.

Confession of A Goddess

She also spoke of Corvinas.
Mihira believes he has made a bargain with the powers of the Hells.
Siath knows he has not.
Mihira does not believe her.
Earlier, Corvinas had told us of seeing his goddess without her blindfold. I had been troubled by the image. Now I had something worse to consider: an accusation against my friend, maintained even when another goddess disputed it.
I agreed with Siath about him.
I follow Mihira. I have sung her story. I know something of what her love and her unblinding mean.
I also know Corvinas.
I have watched him stand by us, suffer beside us, die, return, and continue. Whatever has marked him, I will not casually call it a willing bargain.
It seems even in heaven one may need to insist upon the evidence.
Then my time was ending.
Siath allowed me one question.
I asked where we could find the Wand of Wells.
There were other questions. You may imagine them. I have had ample practice.
She said we would find the wand, and our missing companion, in the Hells.
Then I was back in my body.
“Oh, hell,” I said.
For once, the expression was exact.

A Voice From Hell

Corvinas had heard from Bridget.
I learned of his encounter afterward. I am setting down what he reported; I did not hear her myself.
There had been burning at the mark on his forehead. Flashes of the Hells. Then her message.
It was hot.
She was being tortured.
Salazar had captured her.
She wanted us to hurry up and come and get her.
She also found time to instruct him to keep the bard under control.
That last part made me want to laugh.
I still do not quite know what to do with the feeling that followed.
We buried her. I poured beer into the earth. I sang for her because it was the thing I could give her, and because I could not bear to give her nothing.
I have been learning how to miss her.
Now there is a request.
Come and get me.
I will be careful. I know enough of enemies and deception to understand the usefulness of a familiar voice. But Corvinas’s account and Siath’s answer point toward the same place.
Bridget is there.
And somewhere in all that heat and suffering, she still considered my behaviour a matter requiring supervision.
I should very much like to be present when she delivers her next complaint.

Losing Faith

Mikani woke troubled too.
I cannot set down the particulars of her vision. She has not given me the whole of it, and I will leave that space for her words.
Her worry for the Celestial Isles has been with us a long time. She needs to know what is happening to her home. We keep finding other dangers, other tasks, other urgent reasons to continue, while the answers she needs remain out of reach.
Now her faith is faltering.
There is a cost to that for her beyond an unhappy morning. Something in the divine power she relies upon is slipping away.
I would like to know what the gods expect a person to do with unanswered fear.
I have been managing mine with sarcasm, music, and a degree of personal charm that deserves considerably more recognition. Mikani has been praying.
When asked whether she was all right, she said she was unsure.
I have seldom heard a more reasonable answer.
I wanted to offer something useful. A reassurance I could stand behind. Some promise that her home would be safe, that she would receive an answer, that the power would return.
I had none ready.
She deserves better than having me rhyme around the omission.

Standing Watch

During the last watch, Gildas examined the magic around our camp while I wrote.
He found no threatening presence.
I kept writing.
By morning I had almost decided to tell the others about Siath. I began to speak. There was a question about our destination, about passage and supplies, and the moment moved on.
When they returned to me, I said it was nothing.
They noticed.
Of course they noticed. I have spent our entire acquaintance ensuring that my presence is audible. Sudden restraint was never going to pass for a natural development.
Someone said I would tell them when I was ready.
They gave me room.
That kindness has made it harder to excuse myself.
Siath’s warning needs telling. So does what she said about Corvinas. The book was taken from her. Someone concealed the taking. Someone else may know I carry it.
These facts concern every person walking beside me.
I know what secrecy can cost. I have already had that lesson.
Knowing a lesson and behaving accordingly remain, to my irritation, separate accomplishments.

An Unnatural Silence

I have received no reprieve.
I need to remember that when I am tempted to make Siath’s words into one. She spoke of retrieving the book. She corrected what I believed about its arrival in my life. She trusted me with it a little longer.
She made no promise about my voice.
I still know what lies ahead. I am still scared shitless of reaching it.
Yesterday I could boast of a dragon and mean nearly every word. Today I have new facts, a friend calling from Hell, and another morning in which to decide whether I am brave or merely too far committed to admit the scale of my foolishness.
The others are studying the map.
We need a port, passage, supplies. Hanedal is still ahead of us. The Wells still need tending. Somewhere beyond all of that, Bridget is waiting for us to hurry up.
I can manage the next step.
Before we take it, I ought to tell my friends what happened.
While I can still tell it myself.

A Chess Match I Didn't Ask For

Siath trusts me. For now. How splendid. I wonder whether she knows how much I would prefer a choice.
I will go after Bridget. I will help my friends. I will carry this damned book until I find somewhere it can safely leave my hands. But I am growing very tired of having every person I love become another reason I cannot refuse.
And when I take the next step, some god will doubtless call it devotion.
I should like the record corrected.
I am an unwilling pawn. Let the record show that much.
Yes, I keep walking. I know what waits for me, and I am scared shitless. But Bridget is still in Hell, and my friends still need me. What would you have me do?
Do not mistake that for permission.
I have spent my life putting words to other people’s stories. I would have liked some say in my own.
