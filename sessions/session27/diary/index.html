Session Twenty-Seven - 9/06/26
1832 AS — Namal 28
Secrets Beneath the Monastery

We awoke rested and, for once, relatively intact. Our intention was to seek out Samuel and speak with him further about the Wells. Bedrock remained concerned because several of the books concerning the Wells had apparently gone missing from the library.

Before we could make our way to Samuel, Roon and I noticed two suspicious individuals moving through the monastery. One appeared to be an elf, and one of them was carrying books that looked suspiciously like those we had been seeking. Naturally, rather than doing the sensible thing and immediately finding Samuel, we followed them.

The two disappeared around a corner.

This was particularly irritating since we had been doing a reasonably good job of following them.

A search of the corridor revealed that part of one wall was slightly ajar. Someone had apparently passed through a hidden doorway and had been in too much of a hurry to close it properly. Beyond it was a long dark corridor.

There are few phrases in the adventuring profession more ominous than long dark corridor.

Nevertheless, we entered.

I provided light as we moved cautiously forward. At the far end of the passage was a spiral staircase descending deep beneath the monastery. Several of us noticed that the construction was unusual. Certain stones and tiles protruded slightly from the walls and floor, and there was an unfamiliar symbol above the staircase. Magical examination also revealed traces of evocation magic in the corridor.

In other words, traps.

We proceeded anyway.

Roon took the lead down the spiral stairs and managed to spot two figures below. Unfortunately, armor is not particularly conducive to stealth. They heard us and one of them immediately noticed Roon.

Roon announced that we came in peace.

The hallway disagreed.

The moment he stepped onto the floor below, there was a tremendous mechanical sound and an enormous bolt struck him through the shoulder. This was no ordinary crossbow bolt. It was closer to something one would use to bring down a castle gate. The impact drove him backward and pinned him to the wall.

I believe the lesson here is that when mysterious stones, unknown symbols, and evocation magic all suggest that a hallway is trapped, one should consider believing them.

We managed to free Roon, but the people we had followed escaped deeper into the passage. There were almost certainly more traps ahead, and our quarry now knew we were there. After some discussion we decided that continuing blindly into a heavily trapped underground complex was perhaps not our most intellectually distinguished idea.

We returned to the monastery proper and finally went to see Samuel.

Samuel

We told Samuel about the missing books, the two people we had followed, and the secret passages beneath the monastery. He seemed genuinely troubled that someone had taken the texts without Bedrock knowing about it.

The greater surprise came when we asked Samuel if he could arrange for us to meet the Well.

He looked at us and told us that we already had.

Samuel is the Well.

His true name is Gustan.

For reasons of safety, he has apparently been living among the people of the monastery disguised as Samuel. This revelation makes several previous conversations considerably more interesting in retrospect.

Gustan told us something far more troubling.

He believes that nearly every one of his siblings we have encountered has already been compromised. Korog may be the exception, protected by the Feywoods.

Our discussion turned once again to the Wand of Wells and Salazar. We have suspected for some time that Salazar may possess the wand, but we still have no reliable way of finding him. Gustan confirmed that there are means of reaching the Hells, although confronting Salazar there, in his own realm and on his own terms, would be nearly suicidal.

I am perfectly willing to do foolish things in pursuit of knowledge.

Walking voluntarily into the Hells without a plan exceeds even my standards.

Gustan did provide us with one very important piece of information. The Wand of Wells leaves behind an arcane mark wherever it travels. This is not a physical marking that can simply be seen upon a wall or floor. It exists somehow beyond ordinary material perception, apparently connected to the astral plane.

If we can learn how to follow that trail, we may finally be able to locate the wand.

The missing books are therefore even more concerning than we first believed.

The books do not merely contain histories of the Wells.

They magically track their locations.

Someone possessing those books may therefore possess a means of finding the Wells themselves.

That makes their theft considerably more sinister.

The Battle Below

Our investigation eventually brought us back into conflict with the people operating beneath the monastery.

What followed was a rather substantial battle.

There were six of them in total: an assortment of warriors and killers including a human, a Goliath, a Minotaur, an elf, and others whose precise nature was considerably less important at the time than the weapons they were swinging at us.

They were organized, dangerous, and clearly knew the hidden passages better than we did.

The fight quickly spread through the underground corridor.

I conjured the many blades of Melkior and positioned them among our enemies. The spell proved extraordinarily effective. At one point I moved the whirling blades over two of our opponents at once.

There was a great deal of blood.

I remain quite fond of that spell.

The battle became confused and brutal. The Goliath eventually fell beneath our combined attacks. The Minotaur followed. Several of our companions were badly wounded, and more than once it appeared that someone might not walk out of the corridor.

Still, we held.

The blades continued their work whenever our enemies were foolish enough to remain near them.

One by one our opponents fell.

Eventually only one remained capable of escape. He fled down the trapped corridor at remarkable speed. Even so, several traps triggered as he ran.

We tried to stop him.

I was prepared to use Dimension Door to close the distance, and was able to stop and capture the last "person" although he seemed to not be what he appeared. Calling him human seems like a misnomer.

Five of the six attackers were dead.

None of us were.

I consider that an acceptable accounting, although the survivor troubles me greatly.

Whoever these people are, they now know that we discovered the passages beneath the monastery.

They know that we are investigating the missing books.

And they know that we survived.

Thoughts Before Rest

Today has given us more answers than most days on this journey, but each answer seems intent upon producing several more questions.

Samuel is Gustan.

The Wells may be compromised.

The Wand leaves a trail through the arcane.

The missing books can reveal the locations of the Wells.

And beneath a monastery devoted to discipline and contemplation lies a secret maze filled with traps and armed strangers stealing precisely the knowledge we came here seeking.

Someone has been preparing for this.

The question is whether they have been preparing for the Wells—

or for us.
