Session Thirty-One --- 10/25/26

1832 AS --- Hephal 03

A Town With Excellent Taste

Morek is known for shipbuilding, commerce, literature, poetry, music, and revelry.
I described it to the others as a bardic wet dream.
At last, a geographical observation worthy of my education.
After the monastery, the Gale, and an unsolicited appointment with a goddess, I was ready for somewhere that understood the value of a good song. A place where the books were intended to be read, the drinks intended to be drunk, and the patrons unlikely to announce that my continued suffering served some greater purpose.
Morek honours the goddess of literature. Apollo and Siath also receive their share of attention.
Naturally, she has a presence here.
I suppose it would have been unreasonable to expect an entire town to rearrange its religious affiliations merely because I am having a difficult week.

Divine Assistance, Reasonably Priced

We encountered a travelling merchant on the road.
I approached him for information. He provided some, then opened his wagon to reveal a collection of magical trinkets and several employees who had plainly hoped to remain unobserved a little longer.
An admirable business model. Catch travellers while they still have money, before the innkeepers can reach them.
Mikani wanted an Amulet of the Devout. After the dragon, she was determined to improve her chances of staying upright and keeping the rest of us that way. The merchant produced several divine symbols, with a somewhat firmer understanding of their prices than their properties.
One bore Apollo’s likeness.
I asked about it.
Five hundred gold.
I explained that I was a poor bard, long upon the road, sorely tried by circumstance, and deeply moved by the possibility that such a treasure might finally come within my reach.
A beautiful performance. Intimate. Restrained. Entirely wasted on an audience of one.
Three hundred and fifty.
I should charge admission to my negotiations.
Before anyone accuses me of emptying our purse to improve an already exceptional singing voice, I did offer to leave it if the others thought the expense unwise. We still needed armour and passage.
They agreed to buy it.
When I attuned to the thing, I discovered an Amulet of Bardic Acuity: more inspiration to offer my friends, more strength behind the flourishes of my blade. Three additional measures before the well runs dry.
A very useful purchase.
There is, admittedly, some irony in my recent denunciations of divine interference being followed by the acquisition of jewellery associated with a god.
For the record, I paid for this arrangement. There was a stated price. I was permitted to haggle.
Already an improvement.

A Little Moonlight for Mikani

Mikani found a crescent-moon amulet set with a moonstone. Luna’s symbol.
With a little assistance in the bargaining, that too cost three hundred and fifty gold. It proved to be precisely the kind of amulet she had been seeking, strengthening her magic.
Someone suggested that finding it at a roadside wagon might be a sign that Namaloa still watched over her.
She gave thanks.
And she looked happier.
I could have questioned the route by which one goddess’s symbol became another’s reassurance. I could have offered several observations about gods who allow their faithful to suffer and then receive credit when a merchant has suitable stock.
I kept them to myself.
Mikani had been frightened, unanswered, and losing something upon which she had built her life. Today she found a little hope.
I will not make her carry my quarrel as well as her own.
Besides, she was soon singing again.
The gods remain powerful, though apparently there are limits to what an amulet can accomplish.

The Ring We Did Not Buy

There were rings.
A ring of protection. A copper ring of regeneration. A crystalline ring whose purpose eluded us, although its price was expressed with remarkable confidence.
The copper one could gradually restore lost parts of a person.
Given our company, this prompted considerable interest.
We considered Roon. Corvinas reminded us that he had recently died twice. There followed a discussion of injuries, existing protections, available funds, armour, boats, and whether any of us could reasonably afford another finger ornament.
I asked what sort of music a copper band played.
My contribution was not universally appreciated.
We left the rings.
I record that decision because, should one of us shortly lose an appendage, I expect the ensuing conversation to be lengthy and unpleasant.
Roon argued that we had value to offer aboard a ship: his experience as a sailor, our ability to defend it, my ability to entertain its passengers. We ought not be priced as useless cargo.
Quite right.
Useless cargo rarely saves the vessel and then improves the evening.

An Audition for the Census

At the entrance to Morek, a guard asked our business.
I sang.
He acknowledged that it was beautiful and asked our business again.
A promising town, undermined immediately by administration.
I explained that I was a bard, interested in performing, learning songs, and discovering what the local guild might offer. When asked about my affiliations, I produced my signet of the Knights of Myth Drannor.
I may also have described my companions as my groupies.
This introduced complications when they began discussing their own professions.
The guard wanted to know whether we were bringing goods to sell. I explained that I sold entertainment. Roon had a damaged weapon hilt. Someone wanted armour. We needed somewhere to sleep. We needed a ship.
Eventually we supplied the particular arrangement of words that permitted the conversation to continue.
A vessel large enough for all of us would cost around ten thousand gold.
I calculated that I could probably earn that in a year.
Unfortunately, the end of the world appears unwilling to accommodate a sensible touring schedule.
When Hanedal was mentioned, the guard’s manner changed.
People had gone there. Some had returned. Those who returned had not come back the same.
They had a support group.
I have heard many warnings in my life. That one was unusually persuasive.
He also told us of people who still intended to go: Tanari, Pippi, and their associates, connected with the shipbuilder who owned the harbour. I gave the guard a gold piece for his help.
After what we had put him through, he had earned it.

Two Silver and a Possible Diplomatic Incident

Our enquiries carried us past a ship called the Harpy, where the harbour’s owner was supervising repairs with the vocabulary one expects from a man watching a crow’s nest fall off his ship.
He directed us to the Bleeding Bullywug.
A sailor’s tavern. Worn furnishings, modest purses, and people with more immediate concerns than whether the establishment’s name encouraged confidence.
Five people occupied a corner: a minotaur, a young human woman, a bullywug, and two masked figures.
Roon arranged drinks and an opportunity for me to perform.
I chose a song of Hanedal, then supplied a new final verse about six travellers seeking passage there. The description was sufficiently precise that even someone who had missed our entrance could have identified us.
The larger masked figure noticed first. The woman whispered to the minotaur.
Good. The song had reached the people for whom I intended it.
I finished with a flourish and bowed.
Two silver.
One must distinguish between the quality of an audience’s appreciation and its purchasing power.
The bullywug called something out. I responded in what I hoped was a companionable imitation.
I do not speak Bullywug.
This proved relevant.
There may be a useful lesson here concerning my recent insistence upon the importance of getting languages right. I shall consider it when I have finished being embarrassed.
Mikani joined the entertainment, and the room found the beat. Even a thoroughly drunk patron managed to clap along.
For a little while we were simply making noise in a tavern, with friends close enough to laugh.
I enjoyed myself.
That belongs in this account just as much as anything dreadful.

Passage and a Deadline

Mikani and I approached the people in the corner.
I told them the final verse was true: we were seeking Hanedal.
Then I told them someone dear to me had gone there and failed to return.
That part was a lie.
I am writing it plainly because I will not have this diary confuse a useful invention with what happened. Bridget is in the Hells. I did not explain the Wells, the wand, or the grimoire to strangers in a public room.
Instead, I borrowed the shape of a grief they understood.
The minotaur and Tanari had lost someone themselves.
That made the lie easier to tell and less pleasant afterward.
They had acquired a ship. Roon offered his experience at sea; the rest of us could offer protection. There was room for us, and they agreed to delay their departure until morning so we could provision and rest.
Port seven. First light.
The two masked leaders would remain behind. They had people here to protect and were unwilling to explain more until their companions trusted us.
I could hardly object to the withholding of information while standing there upon a freshly constructed personal history.
They also warned us about the Blood Moon.
Expected within the next few weeks. Being on Hanedal when it came would be a particularly poor decision.
So we now have a destination, passage, and a date by which our bad decision threatens to become worse.
Progress.

Keeping an Eye on Things

We divided our errands.
Mikani, Corvinas, and Rayetta went to the market. Roon, Gildas, and I remained at the docks to examine the activity around the ships.
Roon tried speaking with sailors about goods they might sell directly. His efforts met with considerable suspicion. Evidently a large orc politely offering to pay for things remains too much for certain people’s imaginations.
While we watched, someone began putting up notices after speaking with the harbour’s owner.
I sent my Arcane Eye after the owner.
I have been looking forward to using this spell.
There is much to recommend investigating a suspicious ship without having to place one’s actual head inside it.
The eye followed him aboard the Harpy and below deck.
There were books.
Tomes concerning Chaotix.
Then another container, marked with the symbol of the Cult of Elemental Chaos.
I paid closer attention.
A harbour full of ships and goods is one thing. A ship carrying that particular collection is another. I withdrew the eye to watch the gangway and waited to see who came aboard.
This time, when the others found me quiet, there was a practical explanation.
I was doing something useful with my mouth closed.
I trust nobody will grow accustomed to it.

A Fairy With a Reputation

Gildas examined the notices.
A small winged woman carrying a needle-fine rapier. Wanted dead or alive. Called the Sailor of the Giggling Heart.
Last seen approaching Hanedal’s waters from Norhundra.
The accusations included piracy, murder, and attacking a naval officer.
Someone suggested hiring her.
Our recruitment standards are becoming wonderfully straightforward.
I do not yet know whether she is a villain, an ally, or somebody whose principal misfortune was making the wrong enemies. A wanted notice provides the authorities’ account. I should like to hear hers.
The name alone promises a better song than most naval officers could inspire.

Armour, Fish, and a Welcome Change

The others returned with improved armour for Mikani and Corvinas. Each had traded chainmail and a hundred gold for splint.
I approve of any purchase that makes it harder to put a hole through my friends.
They also brought Roon some dragonfish.
Mikani had found a green dragonborn fishmonger with connections to her clan, a man known locally as Ed. The fish came from the Celestial Isles, and she was delighted to encounter a taste of home.
I missed the market meal. Judging by the enthusiasm surrounding the portion presented to Roon, it had provided entertainment as well as nourishment.
He swallowed the whole thing, found it pleasantly spicy, and asked for more.
No dragon killed him. No dragonfish killed him.
An excellent run.
Mikani wanted us to meet Ed later at the tavern with the scrying-eye sign.
Meanwhile, I still had an eye of my own attending to business.

Blue Clothing and Familiar Symbols

Near nightfall, two people in splendid royal-blue clothing approached the Harpy.
I recognised the clothing as belonging to Norhundran nobility. Their particular identities were beyond me.
They spoke with the owner, boarded, and went below. Soon afterward, he handed keys to the harbourmaster, took his place aboard, and the ship departed.
I described what I was seeing to my companions as it happened.
The books. The cult’s symbol. The nobles.
I want to preserve the distinction between those observations and what I suspect. I saw the cargo and the passengers. I did not acquire a complete explanation merely by looking at them.
Still, Norhundran nobles travelling aboard a ship carrying materials associated with Chaotix and the Cult of Elemental Chaos deserve considerably more attention than their tailoring.
The Harpy sailed.
Our own passage remained arranged for morning on a different vessel.
For the moment, we went to find Mikani’s friend.

A Lot of Gold and No Way Home

Ed was drunk.
Very drunk.
The kind of drunk in which a question enters, spends some time wandering about, and returns without the information it went in to fetch.
He was pleased to see Mikani. He was pleased to see us. He seemed pleased by a substantial portion of existence.
His more sober companion explained why.
Ed had made an exceptionally profitable sale.
To wealthy, irritating customers.
After a conversation about the Celestial Isles.
A signet ring had changed hands for something like fifteen thousand gold.
Mikani recognised the kind of ring described. She had one herself. These signets allowed access to the Isles.
Ed had sold his.
Mikani asked how he intended to get home.
It was an excellent question, arriving several drinks later than would have been ideal.
Watching the understanding reach him was painful. He still had the money. Perhaps he could find the buyer. Perhaps he could buy it back.
Perhaps the buyers had just departed aboard the Harpy.
That connection needs confirming. I will not turn a suspicion into a fact simply because it fits so neatly.
But we now have a ship carrying disturbing cargo, Norhundran nobles in a hurry, and a wealthy buyer who wanted access to Mikani’s home badly enough to pay a small fortune for it.
She came here hoping for news of the Isles.
I wish we had found her better news.

More to Say

Today I bought an amulet that gives me more to offer.
More encouragement for my friends. More strength in the turn of a blade. More chances to make my voice useful.
I am pleased with it. Proud of the bargaining, too.
I can be pleased and still know what is coming.
The gods have not become easier to trust because I found something useful bearing one of their symbols. I remain an unwilling pawn, and I have no intention of allowing a successful shopping trip to be entered into the divine record as reconciliation.
But Mikani smiled today.
Roon enjoyed his fish. Corvinas has better armour. Gildas helped us read what someone wanted the harbour to know, while I looked where they had not intended us to look. Rayetta is learning what travelling with us entails and has, so far, continued doing it.
We found passage.
We found danger travelling ahead of us.
Another answer. Another reason to hurry. Another nail, driven a little deeper while I am occupied with keeping everyone else alive.
I still cannot tell you whether that makes me brave or the greatest fool ever to negotiate a discount.
Tomorrow we are expected at port seven.
Tonight I have a little time, a voice that still works, and friends who will probably complain if I use it too much.
I think I shall give them something to complain about.
